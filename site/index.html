The problem

Three people, three answers.

The shop keeps a spreadsheet, the courier a paper list, and the pick-up point a notebook behind the counter. When the customer asks where their parcel is, each of them says something different.

Customers call the shop.Nobody told them the parcel arrived, or where.

Nobody knows who had it last.A tick on paper doesn’t say when, or by whom.

Anyone can collect it.A name is easy to guess at a busy counter.

How it works

From the shop’s shelf to the customer’s hands.

  1. 1

    Set up your network

    Run OpenRelay on your own server, then add your shops, your pick-up points and the people who work at them.

    terminal 
    $ git clone https://github.com/openstuff-dev/open-relay
$ cd open-relay && docker compose up -d
  2. 2

    Shops add parcels

    The shop adds the order, picks the point nearest the customer and sticks the printed label on the box. The customer gets a tracking link.

  3. 3

    Scan at every hand-off

    Shop, courier and point scan the label with a phone or a handheld scanner. The customer collects it with their pickup code.

FAQ

Questions people ask first

Is it free?

Yes. OpenRelay is open source under the MIT licence, so running it costs nothing but your server.

Do customers need an app or an account?

No. They get emails and a tracking link, and show their pickup code at the counter, on their phone or on paper.

What do shops and pick-up points need?

A phone with a browser, and a label printer for shops. Handheld barcode scanners work too, if you already have them.

Does it work with my web shop?

Shops can add parcels by hand, upload a CSV file of orders, or send them through a small API. Ready-made plugins for Shopify, WooCommerce and Wix are planned.

Where is the data kept?

In your own Postgres database, on your own server. Admins can export a network as one file at any time and import it on another server.
